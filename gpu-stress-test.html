GPU stress test online

A GPU stress test keeps your graphics card busy for minutes instead of seconds. It shows what your device can hold, not just what it can do for a moment.

What a stress test shows

A short run reports peak speed. A longer run reveals three things a short one hides: thermal throttling (the GPU slows to stay cool), unstable drivers (crashes or black screens), and weak cooling (fans loud, frame rate falling over time).

How long should you run it?

  • 12 to 30 seconds: a quick health check.
  • 3 minutes: enough to see most laptops and phones settle to a steady speed.
  • 5 minutes or more: use the Custom option when testing cooling or a new overclock.

How to read the result

Run the Stress test and compare the average FPS with the 1% low. If the live FPS starts high and slowly drops, the device is throttling. If the 1% low is far below the average, you have stutter. If the page freezes or the browser reports a lost context, the driver or hardware may be unstable.

Safety

  • Place laptops on a hard surface so vents are open.
  • Stop at once if the device becomes very hot, smells odd or shuts down.
  • Do not stress-test a phone while it charges in a hot place.
  • This test loads the shader units. It does not fully test video memory, so use a dedicated tool for long hardware validation.
