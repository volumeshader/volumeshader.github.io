Benchmark methodology (v1.0)

Everything the benchmark does, with the formulas used, so results can be understood and reproduced.

Overview

A browser GPU shader benchmark. It renders a procedural 3D volume with a WebGL fragment shader and measures how fast and how steadily your device draws it. It is independent and not affiliated with the original VSBM, so scores must not be compared with the original.

Rendering and shader

One full-screen triangle is drawn. For every pixel the shader casts a ray, intersects it with a bounding sphere, and takes a fixed number of equally spaced samples through the sphere. At each sample it iterates a power-8 Mandelbulb escape-time function to get a smooth density, then accumulates colour and opacity front to back. There is no early exit, so work per pixel stays similar. WebGL2 is used when available, otherwise WebGL1.

Render resolution is the canvas size on screen times the device pixel ratio (capped at 2) times the render scale. Total pixels are capped at about 2.2 million. The exact resolution used is shown in every result.

Timing

Frame times are the differences between consecutive requestAnimationFrame timestamps. The first 1 second of a run is a warm-up and is discarded. Frames longer than 1 second are ignored. Run lengths are 30 seconds, 3 minutes or until you stop, measured after warm-up. Browsers sync to your display, so FPS cannot exceed your refresh rate.

Formulas

  • Average FPS = 1000 x frames / total frame time in ms.
  • 1% low FPS = 1000 / mean frame time of the slowest 1% of frames (at least one frame).
  • Minimum FPS = 1000 / longest frame. Maximum FPS = 1000 / shortest frame.
  • Stability % = 100 x (1 - standard deviation of frame times / mean frame time), limited to 0 to 100. It is a simple evenness score, not a standard industry metric.

The live numbers shown while the scene runs use the most recent 4096 frames.

Reproducibility

During a benchmark run the camera is reset to the default view and input is disabled. Shader, steps, iterations and render scale are fixed per preset. The run is cancelled if the tab or canvas is not visible.

Adaptive behaviour

If two consecutive frames each take longer than 1.5 seconds, the page lowers the preset by one level to keep the device responsive. A running benchmark ends at that moment and the result is flagged as an adaptive step-down. Such results are not comparable with fixed-preset results.

What happens when you run it

Your browser runs WebGL on your GPU, frame times are measured locally, and results are shown in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server. You can export a result as JSON or copy it as text.

Limitations

Results depend on browser, driver, power mode, temperature and display refresh rate. This is a shader benchmark, not a gaming benchmark.
