PC Stress Test Online: How to Stress Test Your GPU in the Browser

Updated 2026-10-21 · 8 min read · by the VolumeShader team

A GPU stress test shows whether your graphics card can keep its speed when it gets hot. You can run one in your browser in a few minutes. This guide explains how long to test, what to watch, how to spot throttling and when to stop.

What a GPU stress test is

A GPU stress test keeps your graphics card busy at or near full load for a longer time than a normal benchmark. A benchmark asks "how fast are you?" A stress test asks "can you keep that speed, and does anything go wrong when you do?"

When people search for a "pc stress test", they often mean testing the whole computer. A browser test like this one mainly loads the GPU. It does not push the CPU or the memory very hard, so it will not tell you if your processor is stable. Use it when your question is about graphics: heat, throttling, crashes in games or a new graphics card.

The test on this site draws the Mandelbulb scene with a very heavy shader. Because the scene depends on math and not on memory, it keeps the shader cores busy, which is exactly what you want for a stress check. You can start it on the GPU stress test page.

Why people run a stress test

  • After building or buying a PC. You want to know that the new card runs under load without crashes.
  • After changing cooling. A new fan, new thermal paste or a cleaned heatsink should show better stability.
  • When games crash or stutter. A stress test can show whether heat or power is the cause.
  • When checking a used GPU or phone. Heavy load quickly shows weak cooling and faulty hardware.
  • After overclocking or undervolting. Higher clocks are only useful if the card stays stable.

A good plan is to use the browser test first, because it takes a minute. If you see a problem, or if you want exact temperatures, follow up with a monitoring tool on your device.

Safety first

A stress test makes hardware hot on purpose. Modern GPUs slow down or reset the driver before they reach unsafe temperatures, but weak cooling, dust and bad airflow can still cause trouble. You run the test at your own risk, so please read this list before you start.

  • Check that your fans spin and that the vents are not blocked.
  • Do not run long tests on a laptop that sits on a bed, a sofa or your lap.
  • Do not stress test a phone while it is charging, in a thick case or in the sun.
  • Save your work and close important apps. A driver reset can close the browser.
  • Stop the test if you hear unusual noises, smell something odd, see colored dots or broken shapes on the screen, or if the device is too hot to touch comfortably.

On the stress test page, there is a clearly marked abort button. You can use it at any time.

How to run the stress test on this site

  1. Prepare the device. Close other tabs, plug in a laptop and make sure the vents are free.
  2. Open the stress test page.
  3. Choose a resolution. For a PC with a dedicated graphics card, 1024 or 1440 is a good start. For a phone, use 512 or 720.
  4. Press start and watch the FPS and frame time for the first minute.
  5. Keep watching while the device warms up. Notice if the FPS slowly drops.
  6. Press the abort button when you have seen enough, or when the device gets too warm.

How long should you run it

There is no perfect time, but these ranges work for most people. They are suggestions, not rules.

DeviceShort checkLonger check
Desktop PC with a dedicated GPU2 to 3 minutes10 to 15 minutes
Gaming or thin laptop2 minutes5 to 10 minutes
Phone or tablet1 minute3 minutes, only with a cool device and no charger

Most of the heat related change appears in the first few minutes, because a cooler needs a little time to reach a steady point. After that, the picture usually stays the same until something else changes. If your FPS is still flat after ten minutes on a desktop, longer is not likely to show anything new.

What to watch while it runs

FPS and frame time

This is the main signal. A steady FPS and steady frame time mean the GPU can hold its speed. A falling FPS means throttling. Sudden spikes in frame time mean stutter.

Stability

The test must keep running. A freeze, a black screen or a message about a lost context means the system reset the graphics driver. One reset on a very high resolution can simply mean the workload was too heavy for the device. Repeated resets at a low resolution are a warning sign.

Visual problems

Flickering colors, random dots, stretched triangles or a screen full of noise can point to a failing card or an unstable overclock. A smooth image with a drop in FPS is a different case, and it usually just means heat.

Noise and temperature

Fans that speed up are normal. A strange grinding noise is not. A phone that becomes too hot to hold is a clear sign to stop.

How to recognize throttling

Throttling means the chip lowers its own speed to stay cool. In this test it shows up as a slow fall in FPS while the scene stays the same. As a rough guide:

  • Stable: the FPS stays within a few percent for the whole run. The cooling is enough for this load.
  • Mild throttling: the FPS drops by around ten to twenty percent in the first minutes and then settles. Many thin devices behave like this.
  • Heavy throttling: the FPS drops by about a third or more, or keeps swinging up and down. The device cannot cool itself under this load.

These bands are approximate. The point is the shape of the line over time. A flat line is good, a gentle slope is common, and a steep fall deserves a closer look. The volume shader test guide explains how to read the 1% low value, which also drops when a device throttles.

What to do if your GPU throttles or crashes

  • Clean the dust. Dust in fans and heatsinks is the most common cause on desktops and laptops.
  • Improve airflow. Use a stand for a laptop, add a case fan, and keep the vents free.
  • Update the graphics driver. Crashes and context resets are sometimes driver bugs.
  • Lower the resolution. If the context is lost only at 1440, the load may simply be too high for that device.
  • Return overclocks to default. If the test crashes only with an overclock, the overclock is the problem.
  • Check the power supply. A weak or old power supply can cause resets under load on desktops.
  • Replace thermal paste or pads. On older hardware, dried paste can raise the temperature a lot.

For error messages and black screens, see our WebGL troubleshooting page.

Stress testing a phone

Phones have no fans, so they cool through the body of the device. They also have strict heat limits and will slow down early to protect the battery and your hand. That is normal and it is the reason a phone test should be short.

  • Use 512 or 720 resolution.
  • Run one to three minutes. There is no need to go longer.
  • Take off the case, and do not charge during the test.
  • A drop of ten to twenty percent in a few minutes is common. A very steep drop in under a minute suggests weak cooling or a hot starting point.
  • When you check a used phone, compare it with the same model if you can.

Frequently asked questions

What is a GPU stress test?+

It is a test that keeps the graphics card under heavy load for some time to see if it stays fast and stable. It helps you find heat problems, throttling and crashes.

Is a pc stress test online accurate?+

It is accurate for what it measures, which is how the GPU behaves under heavy shader load in a browser. It cannot read temperatures or test the CPU and memory, so use it as a quick check and not as a full validation.

How long should I run a GPU stress test?+

Two to three minutes is a good short check for a desktop, and ten to fifteen minutes is a longer check. Phones should run one to three minutes only.

Can a stress test damage my GPU?+

Modern GPUs have protection that slows them down before they get dangerous. Damage is unlikely on healthy, clean hardware, but poor cooling and dust raise the risk. Stop the test if the device gets very hot.

Why did my screen go black during the test?+

The most common reason is a driver reset, because a frame took too long. Try a lower resolution and update the driver. If it happens again at a low setting, check the cooling and the power supply.

Does the stress test also test my CPU?+

Not in a meaningful way. The browser test loads the GPU. Use a CPU specific tool if you need to test the processor.

Is it safe to stress test my phone?+

Short tests are fine on a cool phone that is not charging. Avoid long tests, thick cases and hot places, and stop if the phone becomes too hot to hold.
