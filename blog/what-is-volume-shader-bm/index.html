What Is Volume Shader BM (volumeshader_bm)? A Simple Guide

Updated 2026-10-21 · 11 min read · by the VolumeShader team

Volume Shader BM, also written as volumeshader_bm or VSBM, is a free browser test that pushes your graphics card hard and draws a 3D fractal while doing it. This guide explains what it is, how it works and what the numbers mean, without any heavy jargon.

The short answer

Volume Shader BM is a web page that asks your graphics card to do a very large amount of math and shows you how well it keeps up. While it works, it draws a glowing 3D fractal shape that slowly spins on the screen. You do not install anything. You open the page, press start, and watch the numbers.

The name looks odd, so here is how to read it. "Volume shader" describes how the picture is made. "BM" is most often read as short for benchmark. You will also see it written as volumeshader_bm, volume shader bm, volumeshaderbm or VSBM. They all point to the same idea: a browser test that makes a GPU render a heavy volumetric scene and reports how fast it can do it.

If you only want to try it, open the live test on the home page. If you want to understand what is happening behind the screen, keep reading. Everything below is written in plain language.

What you actually see on the screen

The shape in the middle of the screen is called a Mandelbulb. It is a 3D cousin of the famous Mandelbrot fractal. A fractal is a shape that keeps showing new detail the closer you look at it. The Mandelbulb has bumps, folds and tiny curls on its surface, and the test lights it with soft colors so those details are easy to see.

Most 3D games draw objects made of triangles. A character or a car is a mesh of thousands of small flat pieces. The Mandelbulb in this test has no triangles at all. It is defined by a formula, and the GPU has to work out where the surface is, one pixel at a time. That is why the test is so demanding. The surface is not stored anywhere. It must be calculated again for every pixel, in every frame.

On this site you also get a few extra shapes in the preset menu, such as a Julia style bulb, a cosine field and a twisted torus. They use the same drawing method with a different formula, so you can compare how your GPU handles each one.

Why it is called volume shader, BM and VSBM

Each part of the name has a simple meaning.

  • Volume points to volumetric rendering. Instead of drawing flat surfaces, the program looks through a region of 3D space and decides what is inside it.
  • Shader is the name for a small program that runs on the GPU. The heavy work in this test happens inside a single fragment shader, which runs once for every pixel on the screen.
  • BM is usually read as benchmark. A benchmark is a repeatable test that gives a number you can compare with another device.
  • VSBM is simply the short form of "volume shader BM".

The original public page for this kind of test lives on the GitHub Pages site of a developer who uses the name cznull. Many people find the test by searching for that name, for the address cznull.github.io/vsbm, or for words like "volume shader" and "bm test". We explain the original page and how this site relates to it in a separate post, cznull, cznull.github.io/vsbm and the original volumeshader_bm page.

How the test works, step by step

You do not need to know any graphics programming to follow the idea. Here is the process in six small steps.

  1. The page draws one big rectangle. It covers the whole canvas. There is nothing else in the scene to draw.
  2. The GPU runs a small program for every pixel. A canvas at 1024 by 1024 has more than one million pixels, so that program runs more than one million times per frame.
  3. Each pixel sends out an imaginary ray. Think of a thin laser line that starts at the camera and travels into the scene.
  4. The ray moves forward in small steps. At each step the program asks the fractal formula: "Is this point inside the shape yet?" The shader in this test allows up to about one thousand steps per ray.
  5. When the ray hits the shape, the program refines the hit point. It uses a short search to find the surface more exactly, then works out the direction the surface is facing so it can add light and color.
  6. The frame is shown and the next one starts. The page counts how long each frame took and turns that into FPS.

The formula in step four is where most of the time goes. For every single sample, the fractal formula loops several times and uses expensive math such as sine, cosine, arc tangent, arc cosine and a power function. Multiply that by many steps and many pixels, and you get a very large amount of arithmetic per frame. You can read the details, with the actual code, in our post on volumetric shaders and raymarching.

Why it can be harder on a GPU than a game

A modern game spends a lot of its time moving data around. It reads textures, writes to buffers and handles many different effects. The Volume Shader BM scene does almost the opposite. It reads almost no data and spends nearly all its time on math. This is often called a compute heavy or ALU heavy workload. ALU stands for arithmetic logic unit, the part of a GPU core that does the calculations.

That has two practical effects.

  • It shows raw shader speed. Because memory is hardly involved, the result depends mostly on how fast the shader cores can calculate. This makes it a useful quick check for comparing two devices.
  • It creates a lot of heat. When the cores stay busy for a long time, the chip warms up. Phones and thin laptops do not have much room for cooling, so they may slow themselves down after a while. This slowdown is called thermal throttling, and the test makes it easy to notice.

Keep in mind that a game result and this result do not always match. A card with fast memory but average shader math may feel great in games and look average here. The other way around is also possible. Use this test as one data point, not as the final word.

The numbers you get from the test

While the test runs, the page shows live numbers. When you finish a 20 second run on this site, you also get a score card. Here is what each number means.

NumberWhat it tells you
FPSFrames per second, right now. Higher is better.
Frame timeHow many milliseconds one frame took. Lower is better. 16.7 ms matches 60 FPS.
Average FPSThe overall speed during the run.
1% low FPSThe speed of the slowest moments. It shows stutter that the average hides.
ScoreOne combined number built from average FPS, 1% low FPS and the resolution you picked.

In the 20 second benchmark, the first two seconds are treated as warm up and are not counted. After that the page records every frame, works out the average, and finds the frame time that 99 percent of frames beat. That slow frame time becomes the 1% low value. The score is then calculated as average FPS times 8.5, plus 1% low FPS times 4.5, multiplied by a resolution factor. The factor is 0.6 at 512, 0.85 at 720, 1.0 at 1024 and 1.4 at 1440.

The second half of that formula is the important part. A device that runs at a steady speed gets a better score than a device with the same average that keeps stuttering. We explain how to read these values in detail in the volume shader test guide.

Who uses a test like this

People use it for simple, practical reasons.

  • Checking a new or used phone. A quick run shows whether the GPU is fast and whether the phone slows down quickly when it gets warm.
  • Comparing two laptops or two PCs. Run the same settings on both and compare average FPS and 1% low.
  • Looking for problems. If a computer that should be fast gives a very low result, the browser may be using software rendering instead of the GPU. The test makes that obvious.
  • Checking cooling. A longer run on the stress test page shows whether performance drops as the device heats up.
  • Learning. Students and hobbyists use it to see what a shader and raymarching look like in practice. The Shader Lab on this site lets you change the formula and watch the result.

What the test cannot tell you

Every benchmark has limits, and it is better to know them early.

  • It is not a game benchmark. It measures one kind of work. Real games also depend on memory speed, drivers, the CPU and more.
  • It does not read temperature or clock speed. A web page is not allowed to see those values. You can only notice their effect, because FPS drops over time.
  • It does not test video memory. The scene uses very little memory, so a card with a small amount of VRAM is not punished here.
  • The browser matters. WebGL goes through the browser and the operating system before it reaches the GPU. Chrome, Firefox and Safari can give slightly different results on the same machine.
  • Background tasks matter. Open videos, many tabs and battery saver mode can all lower the result.

Heavy tests make a device hot. On a phone, a tablet or a thin laptop, keep runs short and stop if the device feels uncomfortably warm. See our safety notes.

How to try it in one minute

  1. Open the home page and press the large start button on the canvas.
  2. Leave the resolution at 1024 for a normal comparison. Pick 512 on an older phone.
  3. Watch the FPS and frame time for a few seconds.
  4. Press the 20 second benchmark button for a result you can copy or save as an image.

Frequently asked questions

What is volumeshader_bm?+

It is another way of writing Volume Shader BM, a browser test that renders a 3D Mandelbulb fractal with a heavy shader to measure how fast your GPU can calculate. People also type it as volume shader bm, volumeshaderbm or VSBM.

Is Volume Shader BM a virus or malware?+

No. It is a normal web page that runs a graphics program in your browser through WebGL. It does not install software. The page can make your fans spin up and your device warm, because the work is heavy, but that is the test doing its job.

What does BM mean in volume shader BM?+

BM is most commonly read as benchmark. The short form is not explained on the original page, so this is an informal reading, but it fits what the test does.

Does it work on a phone?+

Yes, on any phone with a browser that supports WebGL. Use the 512 resolution on older phones, keep the first run short, and stop if the phone becomes very hot.

Why is my FPS so low?+

The most common reason is that the browser is not using your GPU. Check that hardware acceleration is on, update graphics drivers, and make sure a laptop is plugged in. Our troubleshooting page lists the other common causes.

Can this test damage my GPU?+

A normal GPU has built in protection and slows itself down before it gets dangerously hot. The risk is low on a healthy, clean computer, but a weak cooler, dust or a phone in a thick case can make the heat uncomfortable. Run short tests and stop if something feels wrong.

Is a high score here the same as a good gaming GPU?+

Not always. The test measures shader math. Games also use memory, textures and the CPU. A high score is a good sign for shader speed, but it does not replace a game benchmark.
