Troubleshooting

Why is my GPU benchmark FPS low?

A low FPS result does not automatically mean a weak or defective GPU. Browser benchmarks are affected by the graphics API, display timing, device temperature, power state and competing workloads.

1. The preset is too heavy

Extreme is intentionally much heavier than Standard. Test Standard first so you have a baseline before judging a slow result.

2. The device is hot

Heat can cause thermal throttling. A long run may therefore be slower than a cold first run.

3. Battery saver is enabled

Phones and laptops can reduce clocks on battery or in low-power modes. Use a consistent power state when comparing devices.

4. The browser is display-limited

A result pinned to 60, 90 or 120 FPS can be limited by display synchronization instead of the GPU.

5. Another workload is using the GPU

Games, video playback, browser tabs or screen recording can compete for graphics resources.

6. Hardware acceleration is unavailable

If the browser cannot access the normal GPU path, WebGL performance can be dramatically different.

7. Resolution is higher

More pixels mean more fragment-shader work. Window size and device-pixel-ratio can change the workload.

8. The browser or driver changed

Browser and graphics-driver updates can alter WebGL scheduling and rendering behavior. Compare like with like.
