This is a real-time WebGL GPU workload, not a prerecorded video. The benchmark uses a fixed render workload and, when the browser exposes GPU timer queries, measures the time spent on the GPU itself. Phones should begin with Simple and Quick.

What is the volume shader test?

A volume shader is a small program that runs on your GPU and works out how light passes through a 3D shape. The benchmark uses a Mandelbulb, a 3D version of the famous Mandelbrot fractal. For every pixel the shader shoots a ray into the scene, takes many small steps along it, and checks how close the ray is to the surface. This method is called ray marching.

It became popular as a free alternative to heavy desktop tools. It needs no install, works on a phone, tablet, laptop or PC, and gives a result in seconds. People use it as an online GPU benchmark, a quick graphics card test, and a short browser stress test.

This site gives you a clear way to run that kind of test, read the numbers, and decide what they mean for your device. Results are measured locally, so they depend on your browser, driver, power mode and temperature.

All modes and types explained

There are two things people mean by "mode": the difficulty level (how heavy the fractal is) and the kind of test (how long and how it is scored). Naming varies a little between sites, but these are the ones you will meet.

Difficulty levels

LevelWhat changesBest for
SimpleFewest ray steps and fractal iterations. Often a lower render scale.Phones, old laptops, integrated graphics, first-time runs.
StandardBalanced detail. A normal day-to-day check.Mid-range phones, office PCs, most laptops.
AdvancedMore steps and iterations, so edges look sharper and the GPU works much harder.Gaming laptops and desktop cards.
ExtremeMaximum load. Even strong cards may drop below 60 FPS.High-end GPUs and stress testing.

Test types

Timed benchmark

Runs for a set time and reports average FPS, frame time and stability. Use this when you want numbers to compare.

Interactive view

No timer. Drag to orbit the fractal and watch FPS live. Good for looking at the scene or checking one setting.

GPU stress mode

Keeps the load running longer to reveal heat, throttling and crashes. Often called a pc stress test or gpu stress test online.

Quick, Standard, Stress

Quick (about 12 seconds) shows peak speed. Standard (30 seconds) is the balanced benchmark. Stress (3 minutes) shows what the device holds after it warms up.

Custom settings

Some apps let you change resolution, steps and iterations yourself, so you can find the exact point where your device starts to struggle.

WebGL and WebGPU

Most versions use WebGL (broad support). Newer ones may also use WebGPU, which can give different numbers on the same hardware.

Shader types: what each one tests

These terms are often mixed together. They are different workloads, so scores from one do not carry over to another.

TypeWhat it isWhat it stresses

Ray marchingThe technique used here: move a ray forward in small safe steps until it hits something.Fragment shader speed and branching.
MandelbulbThe 3D fractal used as the scene. It is cheap to describe and very costly to calculate.Trigonometry and power functions.

This test runs the Mandelbulb ray marcher only. The others are listed so you can tell which workload a result belongs to.

How to run the test properly

  1. Close other tabs and heavy apps. Keep the test tab in front, because background tabs are slowed by the browser.
  2. Plug in a laptop or phone and turn off battery saver. Power modes lower GPU speed.
  3. Use a current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) with hardware acceleration switched on.
  4. Start on Simple or Standard. Move up one level at a time.
  5. Run it three times and note the middle result. The first run is often lower while the GPU wakes up.
  6. Let the device cool for a minute between runs if you are comparing settings.

The test is demanding on purpose. If your device gets very hot, becomes unresponsive or the fan is loud, close the tab. Short runs are normal use; leaving it for a long time is stress testing and deserves attention to temperatures.

Understanding your results

FPS

Frames per second. Higher means smoother. Most screens cap at 60 FPS (some at 90, 120 or 144), so a result stuck at that number means the GPU has spare power at that level.

Frame time

Milliseconds per frame. 16.7 ms equals 60 FPS and 33 ms equals 30 FPS. It shows lag more clearly than FPS does.

Stability

How even the frames are. A steady 40 FPS feels better than a jumpy average of 50. Low stability points to throttling or background load.

FPS at StandardWhat it usually means
60 or moreStrong. Try Advanced or Extreme.
30 to 60Good. Fine for everyday use and most games at medium settings.
15 to 30Moderate. Typical of budget phones and integrated graphics.
Under 15Limited, or throttling. Check power mode, drivers and heat.

Remember that a browser benchmark measures browser plus GPU plus driver plus cooling. It is a useful signal, not a verdict. Compare devices only under similar power and browser conditions, and never judge a phone as fake from one score.

If the score is low

  • Switch from battery saver to normal power, and plug in.
  • Turn on hardware acceleration in browser settings, then restart the browser.
  • Update your graphics driver (PC) or system software (phone).
  • If WebGL will not start, try another browser or visit chrome://gpu to see its status.
  • On laptops with two GPUs, set the browser to use the dedicated card.

How is this different from the original VSBM?

The original VSBM page was created by a developer known as cznull and is hosted at cznull.github.io/vsbm. This site is independent and not affiliated with it. Here the scene is a lighter ray-marched volume shader, with fixed test lengths, 1% low FPS, a result panel and hardware details. Because the workload is different, numbers from the two pages should not be compared directly.

When this test helps

  • Before buying: run the same test on two phones or laptops in a shop.
  • After a driver or system update: see whether performance changed.
  • Troubleshooting: check if lag is a hardware limit or a software issue.
  • Overclock or cooling changes: use a longer run to see stability.
  • Web developers: confirm WebGL works across devices and browsers.

Frequently asked questions

What is volume shader BM?

It is a WebGL benchmark that ray-marches a Mandelbulb fractal and reports FPS, frame time and stability. The BM stands for benchmark.

Is it a real GPU stress test?

It loads the shader units heavily, so it works as a short gpu stress tester. It does not exercise video memory or every part of the card, so use dedicated tools for long hardware validation.

Can I use it to test my phone's graphics?

Yes. Open it in the phone browser, begin with Simple, and plug the phone in. Expect scores to drop as the phone warms.

Does it send my data anywhere?

The demo on this page runs entirely in your browser and sends nothing. It only reads the graphics adapter name to show you what is being tested.

Why is my FPS capped at 60?

Browsers sync to your screen refresh rate. A cap means the GPU handled that level easily. Move to a harder mode to see its limit.

Can I compare my score with the original VSBM page?

Not directly. The scenes and step counts differ, so use the same page for every device you compare. See the section above for the difference.
