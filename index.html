What is Volume Shader BM?

Volume Shader BM (also written volumeshader_bm) is a GPU benchmark that runs in a web page. The BM means benchmark. The test uses a GPU fragment shader to ray-march a procedural 3D volume: for every pixel it takes many samples along a ray and evaluates a Mandelbulb fractal function at each one, then accumulates colour and opacity.

The scene here is a Mandelbulb, a 3D version of a famous fractal. It is simple to describe and very expensive to calculate, which makes it a good volume shader benchmark. If your GPU keeps the picture smooth, it has headroom. If the picture stutters, you have found the limit.

People use it as a fast graphics test before buying a phone, after a driver update, or when a game feels slow. It also works as a short gpu stress test because it keeps the shader units busy.

What this benchmark measures

This is a shader benchmark, not a gaming benchmark. It measures one specific GPU shader workload in your browser.

It measures

Fragment shader math throughput, fill rate at the chosen resolution, and how steadily the device holds speed, through your browser, WebGL implementation and driver.

It does not measure

Overall GPU performance, gaming FPS, video memory, CPU speed, hardware ray tracing, or native DirectX, Vulkan and Metal performance.

How to use the volume shader test

  1. Open the page. The scene runs on its own and the live FPS shows at the top left.
  2. Pick a complexity level. Standard is the default; start on Simple for older phones.
  3. Rotate, zoom and pan the live view (controls below). During a benchmark run the camera is fixed at the default view and input is disabled, so every run does the same work.
  4. Choose how long to run, then press Run benchmark. Press it again to stop early.
  5. Read the result card: average, 1% low, minimum and maximum FPS, stability, duration, complexity and render resolution.
  6. Use pause (or the P key) to cool the device between runs.

Complexity levels: Simple, Standard, Advanced, Extreme

Each level changes how many ray steps and fractal iterations the shader does, and how sharp the image is rendered. Higher levels cost far more GPU time, so scores are only comparable at the same level. Extreme is very heavy and can freeze weak or older PCs, so the test steps down automatically if frames take over 1.5 seconds.

LevelLoadBest for
SimpleLowest steps, iterations and resolutionOlder phones, budget tablets, integrated graphics, first runs
StandardBalanced, the defaultMost phones, laptops and office PCs
AdvancedMore detail and full resolutionGaming laptops, recent flagship phones, desktop cards
ExtremeMaximum steps, iterations and oversampled resolutionHigh-end GPUs and stress testing

Start low and move up. If Standard already sits under 30 FPS, Extreme will mostly show you slideshow speeds.

Understanding your results

FPS

Frames per second, the headline number. Many screens cap at 60 FPS, some at 90, 120 or 144. A result stuck at the cap means the GPU has spare power at that level.

1% low FPS

The speed of your worst frames. If it is far below the average you feel stutter even when the average looks good.

Minimum and maximum

The slowest and fastest single frames. One bad spike is normal at the start while the GPU wakes up.

Frame time

Milliseconds per frame. 16.7 ms is 60 FPS and 33 ms is 30 FPS. Lower and steadier is better.

Stability

How even the frames are. A steady 40 FPS feels better than a jumpy 50. Low stability points to throttling or background load.

Resolution

The internal render size at the chosen level. Higher means more pixels for the GPU to shade.

FPS at the same levelRough meaning
60 or moreExcellent. Try the next level up.
30 to 60Good for everyday use.
15 to 30Moderate, typical of budget phones and integrated graphics.
Under 15Limited, or throttling. Check power mode, drivers and heat.

A browser test measures the browser, driver, GPU and cooling together. Treat it as a useful signal, not a verdict, and never decide a device is fake from one score.

If your score is low

  • Turn off battery saver and plug the device in.
  • Enable hardware acceleration in browser settings and restart the browser.
  • Update graphics drivers (PC) or system software (phone).
  • On laptops with two GPUs, make the browser use the dedicated one.
  • Close other tabs and apps, then let the device cool for a minute.

How the benchmark works

The page draws one rectangle over the canvas and a fragment shader runs once per pixel. Each pixel casts a ray through a sphere that contains the fractal, takes a fixed number of samples along it, and at each sample runs the Mandelbulb escape-time iteration to get a density. Colour and opacity are accumulated front to back. There is no early exit, so every pixel does a similar amount of work. Everything runs on your GPU through WebGL (WebGL2 when available). Full details, formulas and the changelog are on the methodology page.

Best practices for an accurate score

  • Keep the test tab in front. Browsers slow background tabs.
  • Use the same browser, level and power mode when comparing devices.
  • Run three times and take the middle result. The first run is often lower.
  • Do not run long tests on a hot phone or while it charges in warm places.
  • Close the tab if the device gets very hot or unresponsive.

Frequently asked questions

What is volume shader BM?

A browser GPU benchmark that ray-marches a 3D fractal and shows live FPS, 1% low and stability.

Is the volume shader test safe for my phone or PC?

Normal runs are fine. The test is demanding on purpose, so the device warms up. Stop it if it becomes very hot.

What is a good volume shader score?

Compare at one level. 60+ FPS is excellent, 30 to 60 good, 15 to 30 moderate. The 1% low matters as much as the average.

Why is my FPS stuck at 60?

Your screen refresh rate is capping it. Move to Advanced or Extreme to find the GPU limit.

Is it a real GPU stress test?

It loads the shader units, so the 3 minute run works as a short stress test. It does not fully test video memory, so use dedicated tools for long hardware validation.

Why did my score drop on the second run?

Heat. Phones and thin laptops throttle quickly. Let the device cool and compare again.

Does it send my results anywhere?

No. The test and the numbers stay in your browser.
