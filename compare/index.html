How were these numbers made?

The numbers are rough estimates meant to show the general order of devices. They are not the result of a controlled lab test, and they can differ from what you see on your own device. For real data, run the test on your own hardware. You can also inspect your browser and GPU details on the diagnostics page.

How to use this page

  1. Run the 20 second benchmark at 1024 by 1024 and note your average FPS and score.
  2. Find a device in the table that is similar to yours, for example a phone from the same year or a laptop with the same type of graphics.
  3. Compare the numbers. If your result is close, your device behaves as expected. If it is far lower, check the possible reasons below.
  4. Use the calculator above to get a rough band for your average FPS.

Treat the table as a map with rough borders, not as a ranking that you can quote.

Why scores differ between similar devices

  • Power mode. Battery saver and quiet modes lower GPU speed.
  • Heat. A device that is already warm starts slower and may throttle sooner.
  • Drivers and browser. A new driver or a different browser can change shader speed.
  • Screen refresh rate. A 60 Hz screen caps FPS at 60, which can hide the difference between two fast GPUs.
  • Background load. Other tabs and apps take GPU time.
  • Hardware variation. Two phones of the same model can differ a little in cooling and silicon quality.

Build your own comparison

The most useful table is one you make yourself. Run the same test on each device you own, write down the average FPS, the 1% low and the score, and note the browser and the power mode. Do this once after a fresh restart. In a few minutes you have a clear picture for your own devices, and you can repeat it after drivers or cooling change.

To learn how to read the numbers, see the volume shader test guide. To check what your browser reports about your GPU, open the diagnostics page.

Frequently asked questions

Are the scores in the table real measurements?+

No. They are rough reference estimates, not lab measurements, and they were not collected under controlled conditions. Use them only to get a general idea of where a device sits. Your own runs on your own devices are always more reliable.

How does the percentile calculator work?+

It is a simple tool. It places your average FPS into a band, for example 50 FPS or more, and shows a matching label. It does not compare your number against a large database of real results.

Why does my result differ from the table?+

Browser, driver version, power mode, temperature, resolution and background apps all change the score. A difference of ten to twenty percent between two similar devices is normal.

Which resolution does the table use?+

The table is written for 1024 by 1024. Use the same resolution when you compare your own score with it.

How can I get a better comparison?+

Run the 20 second benchmark on two or more devices with the same browser, resolution and preset, then compare the saved score cards side by side.
