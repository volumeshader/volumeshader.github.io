How to spot GPU throttling

When a graphics chip gets too hot, it lowers its own clock speed to cool down. The limit depends on the chip and the device, so there is no single temperature that applies everywhere. In this test you cannot read the temperature, but you can see the result of throttling as a change in FPS over time.

  • Stable: the FPS stays within a few percent for the whole run, and the frame time line stays flat.
  • Mild throttling: the FPS falls by around ten to twenty percent in the first minutes and then settles. Many thin devices act like this.
  • Heavy throttling: the FPS falls by about a third or more, or keeps swinging up and down with spikes in frame time. The device cannot cool itself under this load.

These bands are rough guides. The shape of the line matters more than the exact numbers.

How long to run the test

Most of the change happens in the first few minutes, while the cooler reaches a steady state. For a desktop with a dedicated GPU, two to three minutes is a good short check and ten to fifteen minutes is a longer one. For a laptop, two to ten minutes is enough. For a phone or tablet, run one to three minutes and do not charge the device during the test.

Stay safe

  • Check that fans spin and vents are free before you start.
  • Do not test a laptop on a bed or a sofa.
  • Remove the case from a phone and keep it out of direct sun.
  • Save your work first. A driver reset can close the browser.
  • Press the abort button when the device is too hot to touch comfortably, or if you see broken shapes or random colors on the screen.

What to do if your GPU throttles

Clean dust from fans and heatsinks, improve airflow, update the graphics driver, lower the resolution and return any overclock to default. On older hardware, dried thermal paste can raise temperatures a lot. A weak power supply can also cause resets on a desktop. The full list is in our guide PC stress test online.

To learn how to read the 1% low value, which also drops when a device throttles, read the volume shader test guide. To get a short score you can save, use the 20 second benchmark.

Frequently asked questions

What does the stress test do?+

It keeps the GPU under a heavy, steady load so that you can watch whether the FPS stays the same or falls as the device warms up.

How long should I run it?+

A short check of two to three minutes is enough for most desktops. Phones should run one to three minutes only. The blog guide has a table with suggestions.

What does a falling FPS mean?+

It usually means throttling. The chip lowers its speed to stay cool. A small drop in the first minutes is common. A steep fall means the cooling is not enough for this load.

Can it damage my device?+

Modern GPUs protect themselves by slowing down or resetting the driver. The risk is low on clean, healthy hardware, but dust, blocked vents, cases and heat can make things uncomfortable. Stop the test if the device is too hot.

Why did the page freeze or go black?+

The operating system may have reset the graphics driver because a frame took too long. Try a lower resolution, update the driver and check the cooling. See the troubleshooting page.
