WebGL Troubleshooting and Error Fixes

Find the problem you see, learn why it happens and follow the fix. These are the most common issues with WebGL benchmarks and stress tests on PC, Mac, iPhone and Android.

1. "WebGL is not supported" or the canvas never starts

Why it happens: The browser has WebGL turned off, the graphics driver is blocked, or the browser is very old.

What to do: Update the browser and the graphics driver, turn on hardware acceleration in the browser settings, then restart the browser. Open the diagnostics page to see whether WebGL is reported at all.

2. "WebGL context lost"

Why it happens: The operating system resets the graphics driver when one frame takes too long. Windows calls this Timeout Detection and Recovery, and other systems have similar protection. The heavy shader can reach that limit at high resolution on a weak GPU.

What to do: Choose a lower resolution such as 512 or 720, close other GPU heavy apps and reload the page. If it keeps happening at a low setting, update the driver and check the cooling.

3. Very low FPS, from 1 to 5, on a good computer

Why it happens: The browser is using software rendering with the CPU, often named SwiftShader or llvmpipe, because hardware acceleration is off or the driver is blocked.

What to do: In Chrome, open chrome://gpu and check that WebGL says hardware accelerated. Turn on hardware acceleration in the settings, update the graphics driver and restart the browser.

4. Black or empty canvas

Why it happens: The shader may have failed to compile on your driver, the context may have been lost, or the page may have run out of GPU memory.

What to do: Reload the page, try a lower resolution, try another browser and update the driver. If you edited code in the Shader Lab, press the reset button.

5. FPS is stuck at exactly 60 (or 120, 144)

Why it happens: Browsers do not draw faster than the screen refresh rate, so a fast GPU hits the cap.

What to do: Raise the resolution until the FPS falls below the cap. Only then do you see how fast the GPU really is.

6. The page freezes or the browser crashes at 1440

Why it happens: The load is too heavy for the device, or the graphics memory and driver cannot cope with the long frames.

What to do: Use 1024 or lower. If you really want to test 1440, close all other tabs first and make sure the device is cool.

7. The score is much lower than a similar device

Why it happens: Common causes are battery saver, a hot device, background apps, an old driver or a laptop that uses its weak integrated GPU instead of the dedicated card.

What to do: Plug in the laptop, turn off power saving, close other apps, update the driver and set the browser to use the high performance GPU in the system graphics settings.

8. It works in one browser and not in another

Why it happens: Each browser has its own graphics layer and driver rules, so a bug or a block may only affect one of them.

What to do: Update both browsers, compare the renderer names on the diagnostics page and use the browser that works for your test. Use the same browser when you compare devices.

9. The phone gets hot or the page reloads by itself

Why it happens: The phone slows down to protect itself from heat, and mobile browsers may close or reload a tab to save memory.

What to do: Take the case off, do not charge during the test, use 512 or 720 and keep runs short. Close other apps before you start.

10. A shader compile error in the Shader Lab

Why it happens: A typing mistake, a missing semicolon, a wrong type or a changed function name stops the shader from compiling.

What to do: Read the line number in the error box, check semicolons and decimals such as 8.0 and not 8, keep the function named kernal and make sure it returns a float. Press the reset button to start again.

Frequently asked questions

How can I check if my browser uses the GPU?+

In Chrome or Edge, open chrome://gpu and look at the WebGL lines. On any browser, the diagnostics page shows the renderer name. A name that mentions software means the CPU is doing the work.

Will lowering the resolution fix a context lost error?+

Often yes. A smaller canvas has fewer pixels, so each frame is faster and the system does not reset the driver.

Is a driver update really needed?+

It is a good step when you see crashes, black screens or very low speed. Drivers fix many WebGL problems.

Why does my laptop use the wrong GPU?+

Many laptops have a weak integrated GPU and a stronger dedicated one. On Windows, open Settings, System, Display, Graphics, and set your browser to high performance.
