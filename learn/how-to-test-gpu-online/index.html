How to Test Your GPU Online Safely & Accurately

Step-by-step guide for PC, Mac, iPhone, and Android

Browser-based WebGL testing provides the fastest way to assess your graphics card's health without downloading multi-gigabyte installer packages. However, because browser workloads share operating system resources with other tabs and applications, following proper testing methodology is essential to obtain accurate, repeatable scores.

Phase 1: Preparing Your System

  1. Ensure Hardware Acceleration is Active: In Chrome or Edge, navigate to chrome://settings/system and verify that "Use graphics acceleration when available" is enabled. If disabled, your CPU will attempt software rendering (SwiftShader), resulting in 1-2 FPS.
  2. Close Video Streams: YouTube, Twitch, or video conferencing tabs utilize hardware decoding blocks inside your GPU (e.g., NVDEC or Intel QuickSync), which can introduce frametime variance.
  3. Connect Laptops to Wall Power: Windows and macOS battery profiles cap GPU voltages. Running on battery will cut your benchmark score by up to 50%.

Phase 2: Executing the Benchmark

Open the VolumeShader 20-Second Benchmark. Select your target resolution based on your hardware tier:

  • 512×512 (Mobile Baseline): Recommended for budget smartphones, integrated Intel HD graphics, and battery-constrained devices.
  • 720×720 (Standard HD): Great for ultrabooks, Steam Deck, and mid-tier smartphones.
  • 1024×1024 (Full HD Reference): The official reference resolution used in the Hardware Comparison Database.
  • 1440×1440 (Extreme QHD): Intended for dedicated desktop GPUs (RTX 3060+, Radeon 6700+, Apple M-series Pro/Max).

Phase 3: Interpreting Your Results

Upon completing the 20-second test, examine both your Average FPS and your 1% Low FPS. If your Average FPS is 60 but your 1% Low is below 25, your GPU is suffering from micro-stutters or background task interruptions.
