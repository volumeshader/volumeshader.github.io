How the 20 second benchmark works

The benchmark is a timed version of the volume shader test. When you press the run button, the page does four things.

  1. It starts rendering the fractal at the resolution you chose.
  2. It ignores the first two seconds, which are the warm up.
  3. It records the time of every frame for the remaining time.
  4. It works out the average FPS, the 1% low FPS, the minimum FPS, a score and a tier, and shows them on a score card.

The 1% low value comes from sorting all frame times and taking the one that 99 percent of frames beat. It tells you how bad the slow moments were. A device with a high average but a very low 1% low is stuttering.

Prepare in four steps

Follow these steps so that your score is fair and can be compared later.

1. Close other tabs

Videos, games and many open tabs share the GPU and can cause false frame drops.

2. Use wall power

Many laptops and phones lower GPU speed on battery or in power saving mode.

3. Start cool

Give a warm phone or laptop a few minutes to rest so that heat does not affect the first run.

4. Keep the settings

Use the same browser, resolution and preset every time you compare two runs.

How the score and the tier are set

The score is (average FPS x 8.5 + 1% low FPS x 4.5) x resolution factor. The factor is 0.6 at 512, 0.85 at 720, 1.0 at 1024 and 1.4 at 1440. For example, a run at 1024 with 60 average FPS and 55 for the 1% low gives 758 points, which is tier A. The same average with a 1% low of 15 gives 578 points, which is tier B. The steady device is rewarded.

Tiers start at 950 for S, 600 for A, 350 for B, 180 for C, 80 for D, and anything lower is F. For more examples, read the volume shader test guide.

If the result looks strange

  • Very low FPS: the browser may be using software rendering. Check hardware acceleration and the graphics driver.
  • FPS stuck at 60: your screen refresh rate is the limit. Use a larger resolution.
  • First run lower than the second: this is common. The browser and GPU need a moment to settle.
  • Black canvas or a lost context: see the troubleshooting page.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the benchmark?+

The run takes 20 seconds. The first two seconds are treated as warm up and are not counted. The rest of the frames are used for the result.

Why is the first two seconds ignored?+

At the start the browser compiles the shader and the GPU may be waking up from a low power state. Those first frames are slower than normal and would unfairly lower the result.

What is a good score?+

It depends on the device. Compare with a similar phone or computer, or with your own earlier runs. A steady result with a small gap between average FPS and 1% low is a healthy sign.

Why does the score change between runs?+

Small differences are normal because temperature and background tasks change from run to run. If the results differ a lot, check for heat, battery saver and other apps.

Can I share my result?+

Yes. After the run, use the copy button for a text summary or download the score card as an image.

Can I compare my score with other websites?+

No. Each website uses its own scene settings and formula. Compare only results made with the same test and the same settings.
